"In 65% of online purchase journeys with multiple touchpoints, consumers use Google or YouTube before Meta." — Google / Measure Protocol, 2024. Looking at individual channel performance without a full-path view produces systematically misleading conclusions about what's working.

When Organic Assists Paid — But Gets No Credit

  • Buyer finds client via organic blog → returns via paid retargeting → converts. Paid gets 100% credit.
  • Organic certifications hub builds buyer confidence → buyer searches brand + material → clicks paid exact-match. Organic's trust-building is invisible.
  • Content earns AI Overview citation → buyer arrives to verify → clicks paid ad in same SERP. The qualifying visit gets no conversion credit.
  • Organic brand-awareness queries build familiarity → increases paid brand keyword CTR and CVR. Organic gets no credit for that efficiency gain.

When Paid Assists Organic — But Gets No Credit

  • Paid ad introduces brand to first-time visitor → buyer returns via organic brand search → organic gets the conversion credit entirely.
  • Remarketing ad re-triggers consideration → buyer searches brand organically → organic conversion. Paid's re-engagement gets nothing.
  • Paid validates a keyword converts at high rates → SEO builds organic authority around it → organic credit accumulates. Paid's intelligence informed the investment but receives no recognition.
  • PMax discovers a high-converting query SEO didn't have → organic page built later → organic credit. Paid's discovery gets no attribution.

OBx Source of Truth Framework

Per OBx analytics documentation: "Platform reporting will always aggregate to more conversions than the actual business sees — paid and organic will both claim the same conversion. This is expected and normal."

Small accounts (1–2 channels): In-platform + holistic GA4 view  |  Medium accounts: GA4/CRM as source of truth with last-click recognition  |  Enterprise: Client's internal model or 3rd-party attribution platform

Critical rule: Don't compare platform-to-platform. Google Ads ROAS vs. GA4 vs. Loop-qualified submissions are three different lenses on the same reality. The Integrated Strategy Lead synthesizes — they don't declare a winner.

Section 5 — Meeting Preparation

What to Bring to Every Cross-Channel Strategy Meeting

A cross-channel strategy meeting only works if both strategists arrive with data the other needs — not just their own channel's performance summary. These are the specific data points each strategist should prepare for every meeting where both channels are represented.

🔍 SEO Strategist→Paid Strategy Meeting
What SEO Brings to Help Paid
Data that makes the Paid Media Strategist's decisions smarter — not just a ranking update
Organic CTR by Query
Why Paid needs it: CTR reveals which terms and meta descriptions resonate with real buyers before a click — a paid headline mirroring a high-CTR organic meta title has built-in validation. Also surfaces high-impression, low-CTR queries where AI Overview absorption is occurring and paid coverage should compensate.
Ranking Gaps on High-Intent Terms
Why Paid needs it: When organic ranks #8–15 on a high-converting B-SMART® term, that's an immediate paid coverage gap. Share the exact keyword and current ranking so Paid can close the SERP gap today while SEO builds authority over the next 3–6 months.
AI Overview Competitor Audit
Why Paid needs it: Competitors cited in AI Overviews are winning brand awareness in zero-click moments. Paid can respond with targeted impression share campaigns on those exact queries to ensure the client stays visible even when organic isn't cited in the AI-generated answer.
Top Organic Conversion Pages
Why Paid needs it: Pages appearing most in organic conversion paths are proven landing page candidates. If PMax is sending traffic to a generic homepage, these pages are where ad groups and final URLs should actually point.
Long-Tail GSC Queries
Why Paid needs it: High-impression, low-click queries in Google Search Console are exact-match keyword candidates for paid — especially B-SMART® layered terms that keyword planners don't surface. These are often the highest-converting terms in the category.
Loop ICP Language Patterns
Why Paid needs it: Terms appearing repeatedly in form comment fields are buyer vocabulary — the shorthand, specific grades, and spec language real buyers actually type. These become exact-match keyword additions and ad copy headline candidates no keyword planner will ever surface.
💰 Paid Strategist→SEO Strategy Meeting
What Paid Brings to Help SEO
Data that makes the SEO Strategist's decisions smarter — not just a CPC and impression share update
Search Terms Report
Why SEO needs it: Shows the exact queries the algorithm matched that converted — real buyer searches, not keyword planner predictions. High-converting terms without organic rankings are immediate content build priorities. This is the single most underutilized SEO intelligence source on any account. Share it every meeting.
Quality Score by Landing Page
Why SEO needs it: Quality Score is Google's real-time assessment of landing page relevance per query. A QS below 5 on a page SEO is trying to rank signals a content, UX, or targeting misalignment that needs fixing — regardless of SEO tactics. It's affecting organic performance too, not just paid.
Auction Insights Report
Why SEO needs it: Which competitors are bidding on category terms and their impression share. This is the competitive map of the most commercially valuable queries. SEO uses it to prioritize which competitors to analyze for content gaps and where to accelerate organic authority before a competitor locks in the organic position.
Negative Keyword List
Why SEO needs it: Negative keywords are wrong-fit intent signals — terms that attracted clicks but produced no conversions. These patterns tell SEO which queries to de-prioritize and which buyer intent types to avoid attracting organically. Negative keyword patterns are one of the fastest ways to align both channels on what not to target.
Audience Demographics of Converters
Why SEO needs it: The demographic profile, device mix, location, and behavior of buyers who converted through paid reveals who is actually in the market. Informs organic content depth, format (mobile vs. desktop), geographic focus for location-based content, and publishing timing.
High-Intent Non-Converting Clicks
Why SEO needs it: Buyers who clicked paid landing pages with low bounce rates but no conversion are a UX and content gap signal. If buyers are engaging but not converting on a paid page, the organic equivalent of that page likely has the same friction. Both the SEO Strategist and UX Strategist should diagnose together.

Joint Questions — Ask These Together in Every Strategy Meeting

1
Which high-converting paid search terms don't have an organic page ranking for them — and how long would it take to build that authority?
Paid's search term report reveals what's converting today. SEO's ranking data shows where organic authority is absent. Terms that rank high in both paid conversions and search volume with no organic ranking are the highest-priority content builds in any account. This single exchange can generate a quarter of actionable SEO work in one meeting.
2
Where are we appearing in both organic and paid on the same query — and is double coverage justified or is it cannibalizing budget?
When the client ranks #1 organically AND is spending on a paid ad for the same exact query, the incremental value of the paid spend needs to be evaluated. Sometimes double coverage increases total SERP CTR and is worth it (especially branded terms). Sometimes it's wasted budget where organic is already capturing the click at zero additional cost.
3
Where are competitors outranking us organically on terms where we're already winning in paid — and what's the content investment needed to close that gap?
Auction Insights shows which competitors dominate the paid landscape. If those same competitors also rank organically above the client on the same terms, the client has a dual visibility problem. Paid maintains presence while SEO builds the organic position — but SEO needs Auction Insights data to know which terms are strategically most important to prioritize.
4
What does this month's Loop ICP data tell us about buyer language both channels should be using — are there terms in comment fields neither channel is currently targeting?
Loop form comment fields are the richest real-buyer language source available. Any term appearing three or more times in a month is a keyword candidate for both Paid (exact match) and SEO (page content and meta copy). This question ensures both channels update from the same buyer intelligence simultaneously, rather than working from independently-researched keyword sets that may contradict each other.
5
Are AI Overviews appearing on our highest-value queries — are we in them, are competitors, and does paid coverage compensate if organic is being displaced?
When AI Overviews appear on high-value queries and competitors are cited but the client isn't, Paid can ensure continued visibility through ads that appear above, within, or below the AI answer. The SEO Strategist identifies the citation gap; the Paid Media Strategist closes the visibility gap while organic authority catches up.
6
What does the GA4 Conversion Path report show about how many conversions included an organic touchpoint before a paid conversion — and are we communicating that to the client?
GA4's Advertising → Attribution → Conversion Paths report shows multi-touch journeys. The percentage of paid conversions that had an organic touchpoint earlier in the path quantifies organic's contribution to paid conversion efficiency. This is one of the most persuasive data points for justifying continued SEO investment on paid-heavy accounts — and both strategists should be able to speak to it in any client conversation.
Section 6 — Cross-Channel Metrics

Metrics From the Other Channel That Actually Matter to Your Strategy

Most strategists track their own channel's metrics in depth and treat the other's dashboard as someone else's problem. But specific metrics from the other channel are direct inputs to your own strategy decisions. Here's exactly which ones matter and why.

🔍 Paid Metrics the SEO Strategist Should Track

Metrics from Paid that directly inform SEO content, keyword, and page strategy

Paid Search Term CVR
Which exact-match terms convert at the highest rates in paid campaigns — these are the highest-priority organic page builds. A term converting at 8% in paid with no organic ranking is leaving compounding long-term value on the table every month.Action: Add highest-CVR paid terms to SEO content pipeline as immediate page priorities.
Quality Score by Page
Google's assessment of landing page relevance to specific queries. A QS below 5 on a page you're trying to rank means Google considers it misaligned — fix the content before investing more in organic signals. Low QS affects organic ranking potential, not just paid performance.Action: Prioritize low-QS pages for content depth, buyer language alignment, and UX improvements.
Auction Impression Share
Which competitors dominate paid impression share on category terms reveals who SEO needs to displace organically. High paid competitor impression share typically means they also have strong organic presence — a two-front competitive problem to solve strategically.Action: Run organic competitor content gap analysis specifically on high-auction-overlap competitors first.
Negative Keywords Added
Wrong-fit query patterns Paid identified and excluded. These are organic avoidance signals — query types attracting the wrong buyer intent that SEO should also deprioritize in content strategy to avoid attracting the same wrong-fit organic visitors.Action: Cross-reference with content calendar to deprioritize content built around wrong-fit intent patterns.
PMax Asset CVR
Which creative themes, headlines, and value propositions are generating conversions in PMax. Winning ad copy contains the language that resonates most at the moment of intent — and should directly inform organic page headlines, meta descriptions, and body copy.Action: Mirror highest-CVR PMax headlines as H1 and meta title tests on organic landing pages.
Top Converting Landing Pages
Which destination URLs in paid generate the most conversions — these pages have proven conversion mechanics. Compare against organic versions; if the paid page outconverts the organic equivalent, there's a content or UX gap to close immediately.Action: Audit organic pages serving the same query intent as top-converting paid URLs for missing elements.

💰 SEO Metrics the Paid Media Strategist Should Track

Metrics from SEO that directly inform paid keyword, audience, and landing page strategy

Organic CTR by Query
Terms where organic meta descriptions generate high CTR are producing copy that resonates with real buyers. Mirror this language in paid ad headlines and descriptions for immediate A/B test validation with real conversion data behind it.Action: Use top organic CTR queries as headline candidates in RSA and PMax asset group text.
Organic Ranking Gaps
Terms the client should rank for but doesn't — especially B-SMART® layered terms with clear buyer intent. These are immediate paid coverage opportunities while SEO builds organic authority over the next 3–6 months. Don't let a competitor own the paid slot on a term where SEO is a month away from ranking organically.Action: Build exact-match campaigns specifically for unranked high-intent terms SEO has flagged.
Organic Conversion Path Pages
Pages appearing most in the buyer journey before an organic conversion are proven trust builders — buyers read them to make up their minds. These pages should be the destination URLs in paid campaigns, not generic homepages or category pages.Action: Replace generic paid landing page URLs with the top organic conversion-path pages identified in GA4.
AI Overview Presence
Whether the client appears in AI Overviews on high-value queries — and which competitors are cited instead. Paid must compensate for zero-click AI Overview absorption with visible placements on high-value queries where organic isn't being cited in the AI-generated answer.Action: Prioritize paid impression share maintenance on queries where AI Overviews are absorbing organic clicks.
Long-Tail Impressions (GSC)
High-impression, low-click queries in GSC reveal buyer demand that organic meta copy isn't satisfying. Many are B-SMART® layered terms with very specific intent — perfect for exact-match paid targeting that organic can't capture quickly. Some of the best exact-match opportunities exist here.Action: Pull GSC impression terms with CTR under 1% and add high-intent ones as exact-match paid keywords.
Loop ICP Comment Language
The exact terminology buyers use in form comment fields — the shorthand, grades, and spec language that reveals how buyers describe their need. No keyword planner generates this vocabulary. It's the most validated input for exact-match keyword additions available on any account.Action: Add every recurring comment-field term to exact-match keyword testing in Paid before building organic pages.
The Loop Data Layer — Where Both Channels Get Smarter Simultaneously

Loop form submissions give both channels something neither keyword planner, SERP tool, nor GA4 report can provide: the exact language, industries, roles, volumes, and intent signals of real buyers who already showed up and asked for something. When the SEO Strategist and Paid Media Strategist review Loop ICP data together — not in separate channel silos — both channels update from the same first-party intelligence at the same time. That synchronization prevents both channels from working from contradictory keyword sets, misaligned landing page priorities, and out-of-date buyer assumptions simultaneously.

Section 7 — Strategy Plays

Six Strategy Plays That Require Both Channels to Coordinate

These are the plays where SEO and Paid doing their own thing produces a fraction of the result they'd generate together. Each play has a clear trigger, a specific action for each channel, and a combined outcome the individual channels couldn't achieve alone.

1
Play 1 — Both Channels Coordinate
The Ranking Gap Fill: Paid Covers While SEO Builds
Trigger: SEO identifies a high-converting B-SMART® term where organic ranking is page 2 or lower. Paid confirms it converts in the search term report.
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Prioritize building or deepening the page targeting this term with spec depth, buyer language, and trust signals
  • Set a ranking milestone target (page 1 within 90 days) and communicate it to Paid so they know when coverage can be evaluated for reduction
  • Use Paid's ad copy and headline data as page copy validation — what converts in paid belongs on the organic page
  • Track organic impressions and ranking movement monthly; report back to Paid as coverage rationale adjusts
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Build exact-match or phrase-match paid coverage on the term immediately — don't wait for organic if it's already converting
  • Test 3–4 headline variations using buyer language from Loop ICP comment fields and SEO's organic CTR data
  • Monitor quality score on the landing page — flag to SEO if QS is below 6, indicating content work needed before organic can rank
  • Share converting ad copy back to SEO as headline and meta description candidates for the organic page
⟳ Together
  • Align on the same landing page URL for both channels — creates a consistency signal for Google and better buyer experience
  • Share a combined SERP goal: paid above the fold + organic in top 5 = maximum SERP coverage
  • Review monthly: when organic reaches page 1, evaluate whether paid is still incrementally additive or can be reallocated
Combined outcome: The client maintains SERP visibility on a high-intent term immediately (via Paid) while building permanent, zero-marginal-cost authority over 3–6 months (via SEO). Neither channel alone achieves both simultaneously.
2
Play 2 — SEO-Led, Paid Responds
The AI Overview Compensation Play
Trigger: SEO identifies an AI Overview appearing on a high-value query where the client is not cited — a competitor is. Organic CTR on that term drops measurably.
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Audit the competitor content being cited — identify what format, spec depth, and authority signals earned the citation
  • Update or rebuild the client's page to match or exceed the content characteristics Google's AI is prioritizing
  • Implement IndexNow fast indexing to push the updated content to ChatGPT, Perplexity, and other AI models quickly
  • Monitor impressions and rank movement while closing the citation gap — communicate timeline to Paid
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Increase impression share maintenance on the query while the organic citation gap is being closed
  • Test ad copy that differentiates from what the AI answer covers generically: "DFARS certifications included," "spec sheets available," "same-week ship"
  • Monitor if AI Overview appearance is reducing both organic AND paid CTR — if both are declining, the query may be shifting to zero-click and budget should be reallocated
⟳ Together
  • Agree on a "while SEO catches up" paid spend threshold so budget isn't open-ended
  • Share the competitor citation audit with both channels — what earns AI citation also informs paid ad copy
  • Report to the client as a coordinated strategic response — not two separate channel updates
Combined outcome: The client maintains commercial SERP visibility during an organic citation gap (Paid), while building the content authority needed to earn AI Overview citations long-term (SEO). The client never disappears from high-value queries during organic disruption.
3
Play 3 — Paid-Led, SEO Responds
The Search Term Discovery Play
Trigger: Paid's search term report surfaces a high-converting query the SEO Strategist didn't have in the keyword plan and the client has no organic page targeting it.
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Flag the term to SEO immediately — don't wait for the monthly meeting if it's converting at a strong rate
  • Provide full context: exact query, conversion volume, conversion rate, and which landing page it's currently using
  • Continue covering the term in paid while SEO builds organic authority — don't cut the paid term just because SEO is now targeting it
  • Test ad copy variations on the term to validate which framing converts best before SEO uses it for the organic page
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Add the term to the content pipeline as a priority — Paid has de-risked it by validating it converts before the organic investment is made
  • Cross-reference with Loop ICP comment field language to confirm real buyer vocabulary alignment
  • Build the page around the same buyer intent Paid validated — use winning ad headlines as page H1 and meta title candidates
  • Target the same landing page URL Paid is already using if possible — shared URL creates authority continuity
⟳ Together
  • This play turns Paid into SEO's most effective content prioritization tool — Paid validates conversion before SEO invests in building
  • Document every discovery play in a shared tracking sheet so both channels can measure when organic begins covering what Paid discovered
  • Report the discovery to the Integrated Strategy Lead as a Loop ICP confirmation — if paid is converting on it, buyers are likely mentioning it in form comments too
Combined outcome: Paid's real-time conversion data de-risks SEO's content investment decisions. SEO builds permanent authority around terms already proven to convert. The account builds a self-reinforcing loop where paid funds organic content strategy.
4
Play 4 — Both Channels Coordinate
The Wrong-Fit Filtration Play
Trigger: Loop ICP shows a recurring pattern of low-quality, wrong-fit submissions (hobbyist buyers, wrong industry, below-minimum order size). Both paid and organic traffic are contributing to the wrong-fit volume.
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Extract wrong-fit language from Loop comment fields and add to the negative keyword list immediately
  • Add minimum quantity qualifiers to ad copy: "Industrial quantities · 50 lb minimum" — filters wrong-fit buyers before they click and waste budget
  • Review audience exclusions — if the wrong-fit submissions skew a demographic segment, add it as a PMax exclusion
  • Report cost-per-wrong-fit-lead to the Integrated Strategy Lead as a quantified efficiency gain when volume reduces
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Add minimum order callouts, volume pricing tiers, and industrial-context language to product and category pages to filter wrong-fit organic searchers before they submit
  • Review which pages are attracting wrong-fit organic traffic — if a generic product page attracts retail buyers, refocus the content on industrial and procurement-level intent
  • Add FAQ content addressing minimum orders, lead times, and procurement requirements — self-selects wrong-fit buyers out before they submit
⟳ Together
  • Both channels adjust from the same Loop ICP wrong-fit pattern data — ensuring filtration applies to both organic and paid traffic simultaneously
  • Brief the Integrated Strategy Lead and client together: "We identified a recurring wrong-fit submission pattern and here's what both channels changed." This is a Heartbeat-positive conversation.
  • Measure the change in Loop ICP submission quality score month-over-month as the coordinated filtration takes effect
Combined outcome: The client's sales team receives higher-quality inquiries from both channels simultaneously. Paid reduces budget waste. SEO improves content relevance signals. The client sees a measurable improvement in lead quality — not just lead volume.
5
Play 5 — SEO-Led, Paid Amplifies
The Content Authority + Paid Amplification Play
Trigger: SEO builds a high-quality authority content piece (industry hub, compliance guide, material spec deep-dive) that earns strong engagement and early ranking signals but has low direct conversion volume on its own.
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Build the content to earn E-E-A-T signals: first-hand expertise, original data from Loop ICP patterns, specific buyer use cases the client has actually served
  • Structure with conversion pathways — the authority content should link to product pages and include CTAs, not just exist as standalone informational content
  • Track engagement rate and scroll depth — strong signals to Google that the content deserves ranking even before it converts directly
  • Pass the content URL and engagement metrics to Paid as a remarketing audience candidate
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Build a remarketing audience from visitors to the authority content page — these buyers have demonstrated research intent beyond a standard product page visit
  • Serve retargeting ads to this authority content visitor audience with a stronger commercial CTA: "Ready to request a quote?" — they've already been educated
  • Test using the authority content as a paid landing page for specific informational queries — buyers searching "DFARS titanium requirements" may convert better landing on the compliance guide than a product page
⟳ Together
  • The organic content creates the buyer education layer. Paid monetizes the engaged audience that organic content produces. Neither channel alone completes the loop.
  • Report authority content performance as a combined metric: organic engagement + paid retargeting conversions from that audience = the true value of the content investment
  • If the authority content earns AI Overview citation, Paid should immediately build presence on the cited query to capture commercial intent the zero-click citation creates
Combined outcome: SEO's content investment generates a qualified, already-educated audience that Paid converts at higher rates and lower CPAs than cold traffic. The content earns organic authority AND fuels paid conversion efficiency — two returns from one investment.
6
Play 6 — Both Channels Coordinate
The ICP Expansion Play
Trigger: Loop ICP data shows a new industry or company type appearing in form submissions the client wasn't deliberately targeting — an unplanned inbound demand signal worth capitalizing on.
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Validate the industry demand — check GSC for impressions on industry-specific queries, run SERP analysis, and assess whether the client can actually serve this vertical well
  • Build a dedicated industry landing page if validation confirms demand: industry-specific applications, relevant certifications, case study language drawn from Loop submission patterns
  • Add industry-specific B-SMART® keyword clusters: material + application + industry + requirement combinations that this vertical's buyers actually search
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Launch a targeted paid campaign for the new vertical while the organic page is being built — inbound demand signals mean buyers are already searching
  • Use the Loop ICP language from the new industry's submissions in ad copy — mirror the terminology these buyers used in their form comments
  • Build a Customer Match audience from the new industry's submitters — signals to PMax what the ICP profile looks like for this vertical
⟳ Together
  • Validate with the Integrated Strategy Lead and client before full investment — can the client actually serve this industry? Are the margins right?
  • Share a 90-day build plan: Paid launches immediately, SEO builds organic authority over the next two quarters
  • Report the expansion to the client as Loop ICP intelligence working: "Our form data identified organic demand from this industry we weren't targeting — here's how both channels responded."
Combined outcome: An unplanned inbound demand signal from Loop gets converted into a deliberate, dual-channel expansion play within 30–60 days — informed entirely by real buyer data, not keyword planner assumptions.

Partnership Playbook Recap

What Every SEO and Paid Media Strategist Should Walk Away With

These are the principles that define an effective SEO + Paid partnership at OBx. They apply regardless of account size, vertical, or how long you've been working with a client.

01
You Share a Search Ecosystem
Both channels appear on the same SERP, compete for the same buyer attention, and drive toward the same conversion. Optimizing in isolation means optimizing with incomplete information — every single time.
02
Each Channel Sees What the Other Can't
Paid sees query-level conversion data and real-time competitive auction signals. SEO sees long-term authority patterns and SERP landscape shifts. Neither is complete without the other. The exchange is not optional.
03
The Buyer Journey Is Multi-Touch
65% of multi-touchpoint journeys include Google or YouTube before Meta. Buyers research organically, return directly, and convert via paid — or vice versa. Last-click attribution lies to both channels equally and systematically.
04
Last-Click Is a Starting Point, Not a Strategy
GA4's default model undervalues upper-funnel content and over-credits the final click. Use GA4 Conversion Paths, Data-Driven attribution, and Loop ICP form submissions together as a fuller picture of what's actually generating pipeline.
05
Bring Data the Other Channel Needs
SEO brings ranking gaps, organic CTR wins, AI Overview audits, and Loop ICP language. Paid brings search term reports, quality scores, auction insights, and negative keyword patterns. Every meeting. Every month.
06
Every Meeting Must Produce a Cross-Channel Action
A review that produces no actions from the other channel's data produced no cross-channel value. At minimum: one SEO action from paid data, and one paid action from SEO data — every single month.
