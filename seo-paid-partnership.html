Section 2 — Channel Visibility

SEO and Paid Search provide fundamentally different types of visibility into buyer behavior. Neither is complete on its own. The most important question isn't "which channel is performing better?" — it's "what is each channel revealing that the other needs to act on?"

The SEO Strategist's best keyword data comes from the Paid Media Strategist's search term report. When both channels operate on isolated data sets, both channels make suboptimal decisions with incomplete evidence — every single month.

Section 3 — The Full User Path

The Buyer's Journey Is Not a Straight Line — and Your Attribution Model Is Pretending It Is

In B2B and industrial buying, the path from first awareness to form submission is rarely a single session. Buyers research, return, compare, consult colleagues, and then convert — often across multiple sessions, multiple channels, and sometimes multiple weeks. The channel that gets the last click gets the credit. The channels that built the trust get nothing.

The Data Point That Anchors This

"In 65% of online purchase journeys with multiple touchpoints, consumers use Google or YouTube before Meta." — Google / Measure Protocol, 2024. The implication: organic and paid search touchpoints are almost always part of a multi-touch journey. The channel that gets last-click credit frequently didn't do the heaviest lifting. Looking at individual channel performance without a full-path view produces systematically misleading conclusions about what's actually working — and leads to budget and content decisions built on incomplete evidence.

When Organic Assists Paid — But Gets No Credit

  • Buyer finds the client through an organic blog post → returns days later via a paid retargeting ad → converts. Paid gets 100% credit.
  • Organic certification hub page builds buyer confidence → buyer searches brand + material term → clicks a paid exact-match ad for the final session. Organic's trust-building is invisible.
  • Content earns AI Overview citation → buyer arrives to verify → clicks paid ad in the same SERP. The citation that drove the qualifying visit gets no conversion credit.
  • Organic ranks for brand-awareness queries at top-of-funnel → brand familiarity increases paid brand keyword CTR and conversion rate. Organic gets no credit for the efficiency gain.

When Paid Assists Organic — But Gets No Credit

  • Paid ad introduces the brand to a first-time visitor → buyer comes back via organic brand search → organic conversion. Paid created the brand awareness; organic gets the credit.
  • Remarketing ad re-triggers consideration → buyer decides to search brand organically → organic conversion attributed entirely to SEO. Paid's re-engagement gets nothing.
  • Paid validates a keyword converts at high rates → SEO builds organic authority around it → organic credit accumulates. Paid's conversion intelligence informed the organic investment but receives no recognition for it.
  • PMax discovers a high-converting query SEO didn't have on its radar → organic page built later → organic credit accumulates. Paid's query discovery gets no attribution.

OBx Attribution Framework: The Source of Truth Principle

Per OBx's analytics documentation: "Platform reporting will always aggregate to more conversions than the actual business sees — paid and organic will both claim the same conversion." This is expected and normal, not a tracking problem.

Small accounts (1–2 channels): In-platform measurement + holistic GA4 view as primary source of truth
Medium accounts (3–5 channels): GA4/CRM as source of truth with recognition of last-click limitations
Enterprise: Client's internal model or third-party attribution platform

The critical instruction from OBx: Don't compare platform-to-platform. Google Ads ROAS vs. GA4 attribution vs. Loop-qualified form submissions are three different lenses on the same reality. Use each for what it's good at. The Integrated Strategy Lead's job is to synthesize, not to declare one winner.

❌ Don't Say This About Attribution
These framings create confusion and erode trust
  • "Google Ads shows 87 conversions but GA4 only shows 42 — the platform must be overcounting or there's a tracking bug."
  • "SEO isn't converting — all the conversions this month are coming from paid search."
  • "We should cut SEO budget and reallocate it to paid because paid is showing more attributed conversions."
  • "These numbers don't add up — something must be wrong with the setup."
  • Comparing ROAS across platforms as if they use the same model and the same data
✅ Do Say This About Attribution
These framings build strategic credibility
  • "The difference between Google Ads and GA4 conversion numbers is expected — each uses a different attribution model. GA4 uses last-click as default; Google Ads models additional contributions. We use GA4 trends as our source of truth and Google Ads for campaign-level optimization."
  • "SEO often assists paid conversions — buyers research organically, then convert via a paid ad. Last-click gives paid the credit, but the organic content built the trust that made the click convert. The GA4 Conversion Path report shows this."
  • "The Loop form submissions are our cleanest read on total opportunity generation — they're channel-agnostic. They tell us who submitted, what they asked for, and what the quality looks like regardless of which channel last touched them."
  • "We're seeing both channels contribute to the buyer journey. The question isn't which one is working — it's how we use what each channel is generating to make the other smarter."
Section 5 — Meeting Preparation

What to Bring to Every Cross-Channel Strategy Meeting

A cross-channel strategy meeting only works if both strategists arrive with data the other needs — not just their own channel's performance summary. The following are the specific data points, insights, and questions each strategist should prepare for every meeting where both channels are represented.

🔍 SEO Strategist→Paid Strategy Meeting
What SEO Brings to Help Paid
Data that makes the Paid Media Strategist's decisions smarter — not just a ranking update
Organic CTR by Query
Why Paid needs it: CTR reveals which terms and meta descriptions resonate with real buyers before a click — a paid headline mirroring a high-CTR organic meta title has built-in validation. Also surfaces queries with high impressions but low CTR, signaling AI Overview absorption or a paid ad copy opportunity.
Ranking Gaps on High-Intent Terms
Why Paid needs it: When organic ranks #8–15 on a high-converting B-SMART® term, that's an immediate paid coverage gap. Brings the exact keyword and current ranking position so Paid can close the SERP gap today while organic authority builds over months. Don't let a competitor own the paid spot on a term where SEO is a month away from ranking.
AI Overview Competitor Audit
Why Paid needs it: Competitors cited in AI Overviews are winning brand awareness in the zero-click moment. Paid can respond with targeted impression share campaigns on those exact queries to ensure the client is still visible even when organic isn't being cited in the AI-generated answer.
Top Organic Conversion Pages
Why Paid needs it: Pages appearing most frequently in organic conversion paths are proven landing page candidates. If PMax is currently sending traffic to a generic homepage or category page, these organic conversion-path pages are where ad groups and final URLs should actually point.
Long-Tail Query Patterns (GSC)
Why Paid needs it: Long-tail queries in Google Search Console — high impressions, low clicks — are exact-match keyword candidates for paid, especially B-SMART® layered terms that keyword planners don't surface. These are the most specific, highest-intent terms in the category and Paid should be covering them today while organic builds.
Loop ICP Language Patterns
Why Paid needs it: Terms appearing repeatedly in form comment fields are buyer vocabulary — the shorthand, specific grades, and spec language real buyers actually type. These become exact-match keyword additions and validated ad copy headline candidates that no keyword planner will ever surface independently.
💰 Paid Strategist→SEO Strategy Meeting
What Paid Brings to Help SEO
Data that makes the SEO Strategist's decisions smarter — not just a CPC and impression share update
Search Terms Report
Why SEO needs it: Shows the exact queries the algorithm matched that converted — real buyer searches, not keyword planner predictions. High-converting terms without organic rankings are immediate content build priorities. This is the single most underutilized SEO intelligence source available on any account — share it every meeting.
Quality Score by Landing Page
Why SEO needs it: Quality Score is Google's real-time assessment of landing page relevance and experience relative to specific queries. A low QS (below 5) on a page SEO is trying to rank signals a content, UX, or targeting misalignment that needs to be fixed regardless of SEO tactics — and it's affecting organic performance too, not just paid.
Auction Insights Report
Why SEO needs it: Which competitors are bidding on the client's category terms, their impression share and overlap rate — this is the competitive map of the most commercially valuable queries. SEO uses it to prioritize which competitors to analyze for content gaps and where to accelerate organic authority building before a competitor locks in the organic position too.
Negative Keyword List
Why SEO needs it: Negative keywords are wrong-fit intent signals — terms that attracted clicks but produced no conversions. These patterns tell SEO which queries to actively de-prioritize in content strategy and which buyer intent types to avoid attracting organically. Negative keyword patterns are one of the fastest ways to align organic and paid on what not to target.
Audience Demographics of Converters
Why SEO needs it: The demographic profile, device mix, location, and behavioral characteristics of buyers who converted through paid reveals who is actually in the market. This informs organic content depth, format considerations (mobile-first vs. desktop), geographic focus for location-based content, and time-based publishing and promotion decisions.
High-Intent Non-Converting Clicks
Why SEO needs it: Buyers who clicked specific paid landing pages with low bounce rates but no conversion are a UX and content gap signal. If buyers are engaging with a paid landing page but not converting, the organic equivalent of that page likely has the same friction. Both the SEO Strategist and UX Strategist should diagnose this together, not in isolation.

Joint Questions — Ask These Together in Every Strategy Meeting

These questions require both channels to answer and can't be answered well by looking at one channel's dashboard in isolation. Bring them to every cross-channel meeting as shared agenda items.

1
Which high-converting paid search terms don't have an organic page ranking for them — and how long would it take to build that authority?
Paid's search term report reveals what's converting today. SEO's ranking data shows where organic authority is absent. Terms that rank high in both conversions (Paid) and search volume (GSC) with no organic ranking are the highest-priority content builds in any account. This one exchange between channels can generate a quarter of actionable SEO work in one meeting.
2
Where are we appearing in both organic and paid on the same query — and is the double coverage justified or is it cannibalizing budget?
When the client ranks #1 organically AND is spending on a paid ad for the same exact query, the incremental value of the paid spend needs to be evaluated. Sometimes double coverage increases total SERP CTR and is worth it (especially for branded terms). Sometimes it's wasted budget where organic is already capturing the click at zero additional cost. This can only be evaluated when both channels have visibility into each other's performance.
3
Where are competitors outranking us organically on terms where we're already winning in paid — and what's the content investment needed to close that gap?
Auction Insights (from Paid) shows which competitors are dominating the paid landscape on specific terms. If those same competitors also rank organically above the client on those terms, the client has a dual visibility problem. Paid can maintain presence while SEO builds the organic position — but the SEO Strategist needs the Auction Insights data to know which terms are strategically the most important to prioritize.
4
What does the Loop ICP data from this month's form submissions tell us about the buyer language both channels should be using — and are there terms appearing in comment fields that neither channel is currently targeting?
Loop form comment fields are the richest real-buyer language source available. Any term that appears three or more times in a month is a keyword candidate for both Paid (exact match) and SEO (page content and meta copy). This question ensures both channels are updated from the same buyer intelligence simultaneously, rather than each channel working from its own independently-researched keyword set that may contradict each other.
5
Are AI Overviews appearing on any of our highest-value queries — and are we in them, are competitors in them, and does paid coverage compensate if organic is being displaced?
OBx's SERP analysis guidance explicitly instructs strategists to evaluate AI Overview presence as part of every SERP audit. When AI Overviews appear on high-value queries and competitors are cited but the client isn't, Paid can ensure continued visibility through ads that appear above, within, or below the AI answer. The SEO Strategist identifies the citation gap; the Paid Media Strategist closes the visibility gap while organic authority catches up.
6
What is the GA4 Conversion Path data showing about how many conversions included an organic touchpoint before a paid conversion — and are we communicating that in our reports?
GA4's Advertising → Attribution → Conversion Paths report shows multi-touch journeys. Looking at the percentage of paid conversions that had an organic touchpoint earlier in the path quantifies organic's contribution to paid conversion efficiency. This is one of the most persuasive data points for justifying continued SEO investment on accounts that are heavily paid-dependent — and both strategists should be able to speak to it in any client conversation.

The Loop Data Layer — Where Both Channels Get Smarter Simultaneously

Loop form submissions give both channels something neither keyword planner, SERP tool, nor GA4 report can provide: the exact language, industries, roles, volumes, and intent signals of real buyers who already showed up and asked for something. When the SEO Strategist and Paid Media Strategist review Loop ICP data together — not in separate channel silos — both channels update from the same first-party intelligence at the same time. That synchronization is what prevents the two channels from working from contradictory keyword sets, misaligned landing page priorities, and out-of-date buyer assumptions simultaneously.

Section 7 — Strategy Plays

Six Strategy Plays That Require Both Channels to Coordinate

These are the plays where SEO and Paid doing their own thing produces a fraction of the result they'd generate together. Each play has a clear trigger that tells you when to run it, a specific action for each channel, and what the combined outcome should be.

1
Play 1 — Both Channels Coordinate
The Ranking Gap Fill: Paid Covers While SEO Builds
Trigger: SEO identifies a high-converting B-SMART® term where organic ranking is page 2 or lower. Paid confirms it converts in search term data.
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Prioritize building or deepening the page targeting this term — spec depth, buyer language, trust signals
  • Set a ranking milestone target (page 1 within 90 days) and communicate it to Paid so they know when paid coverage can be evaluated for reduction
  • Use Paid's ad copy and headline data as page copy testing validation — what's converting in paid belongs on the organic page
  • Track organic impressions and ranking movement on the term monthly; report back to Paid as coverage rationale adjusts
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Build exact-match or phrase-match paid coverage on the term immediately — don't wait for organic to catch up if the term is converting
  • Test 3–4 headline variations using the buyer language from Loop ICP comment fields and SEO's organic CTR data
  • Monitor quality score on the landing page — flag to SEO if QS is below 6, indicating the page needs content work before organic can rank well
  • Share converting ad copy back to SEO as headline and meta description test candidates for the organic page
⟳ Together
  • Align on the same landing page URL for both channels — creates a consistency signal for Google and a better buyer experience
  • Share a combined SERP presence goal: paid above the fold + organic in top 5 = maximum SERP coverage on this query
  • Review monthly: when organic reaches page 1, evaluate whether paid is still incrementally additive or can be reallocated
Combined outcome: The client maintains SERP visibility on a high-converting, high-intent term immediately (via Paid) while building permanent, zero-marginal-cost authority over 3–6 months (via SEO). Neither channel alone achieves both simultaneously.
2
Play 2 — SEO-Led, Paid Responds
The AI Overview Compensation Play
Trigger: SEO identifies that an AI Overview is appearing on a high-value query and the client is not cited — a competitor is. Organic CTR on that term drops measurably.
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Audit the competitor content being cited — identify what format (spec depth, structured data, authority signals) earned the citation
  • Update or rebuild the client's page to match or exceed the content characteristics Google's AI is prioritizing for citation
  • Implement IndexNow / Bing Webmaster Tools fast indexing to push the updated content to AI tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity as quickly as possible
  • Monitor impressions and rank movement while the citation gap is being closed — communicate timeline estimate to Paid
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Increase impression share maintenance on the query while the organic citation gap is being closed — ads appearing above or below the AI Overview keep the client visible
  • Test ad copy that matches the content depth AI Overviews are providing — "DFARS certifications included," "spec sheets available" — to differentiate from what the AI answer covers generically
  • Monitor if the AI Overview appearance is reducing organic CTR AND paid CTR — if both are declining, the query's intent may be shifting to zero-click and budget should be reallocated elsewhere
⟳ Together
  • Agree on a "while SEO catches up" paid spend threshold so budget isn't open-ended
  • Share the competitor citation audit with both channels — what's earning AI citation on content also reveals what should be in paid ad copy
  • Report to the client as a coordinated strategic response, not two separate channel updates
Combined outcome: The client maintains commercial SERP visibility during an organic citation gap (Paid), while building the content authority needed to earn AI Overview citations long-term (SEO). The client never disappears from high-value queries, even during organic disruption.
3
Play 3 — Paid-Led, SEO Responds
The Search Term Discovery Play
Trigger: Paid's search term report surfaces a high-converting query the SEO Strategist didn't have in the keyword plan and the client has no organic page targeting it.
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Flag the term from the search term report to SEO immediately — don't wait for the monthly meeting if it's converting at a strong rate
  • Provide the full context: exact query, conversion volume, conversion rate, and which landing page it's currently going to
  • Continue covering the term in paid while SEO builds organic authority — don't cut the paid term just because SEO is now targeting it
  • Test ad copy variations on the term to validate which framing converts best before SEO uses that copy for the organic page
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Add the term to the content pipeline as a priority build — Paid has de-risked it by validating it converts before the organic investment is made
  • Cross-reference the term against Loop ICP comment field language to confirm real buyer vocabulary alignment
  • Build the page around the same buyer intent Paid validated — use winning ad headlines as page H1 and meta title candidates
  • Target the landing page URL Paid is already using if possible — shared URL creates authority continuity across both channels
⟳ Together
  • This play turns Paid into SEO's most effective content prioritization tool — Paid validates conversion before SEO invests in building
  • Document every discovery play in a shared tracking sheet so both channels can measure when organic begins covering what Paid discovered
  • Report the discovery to the Integrated Strategy Lead as a Loop ICP pattern confirmation — if paid is converting on it, buyers are likely mentioning it in form comments too
Combined outcome: Paid's real-time conversion data de-risks SEO's content investment decisions. SEO builds permanent, compounding authority around terms already proven to convert. The account builds a self-reinforcing discovery loop where paid funds organic content strategy.
4
Play 4 — Both Channels Coordinate
The Wrong-Fit Filtration Play
Trigger: Loop ICP shows a recurring pattern of low-quality, wrong-fit submissions (hobbyist buyers, wrong industry, below-minimum order size). Both paid and organic traffic are contributing to the wrong-fit volume.
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Extract wrong-fit language from Loop comment fields ("small amounts," "personal project," "just need a few pieces") and add to negative keyword list immediately
  • Add minimum quantity qualifiers to ad copy: "Industrial quantities · 50 lb minimum" — filters wrong-fit buyers before they click and waste budget
  • Review audience exclusions — if demographics from the wrong-fit submissions skew a particular segment, add it as an audience exclusion to PMax
  • Report the cost-per-wrong-fit-lead to the Integrated Strategy Lead as a quantified efficiency gain when the negative keywords reduce that volume
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Add minimum order callouts, volume pricing tiers, and industrial-context language to product and category pages to filter wrong-fit organic searchers before they submit
  • Review which pages are attracting wrong-fit organic traffic — if a generic "stainless steel bar" page is attracting retail buyers, refocus the content on industrial and procurement-level intent
  • Add FAQ content addressing minimum orders, lead times, and procurement requirements — answers the wrong-fit buyer's questions in a way that self-selects them out before they submit
⟳ Together
  • Both channels adjust from the same Loop ICP wrong-fit pattern data — ensuring the filtration applies to both organic and paid traffic simultaneously, not just one channel
  • Brief the Integrated Strategy Lead and client together: "We identified a recurring wrong-fit submission pattern and here's what we changed in both channels to reduce it." This is a Heartbeat-positive conversation.
  • Measure the change in Loop ICP submission quality score month-over-month as the coordinated filtration takes effect
Combined outcome: The client's sales team receives higher-quality inquiries from both channels simultaneously. Paid reduces budget waste. SEO improves content relevance signals. The client sees a measurable improvement in lead quality — not just lead volume — which is a qualitatively different and more valuable outcome.
5
Play 5 — SEO-Led, Paid Amplifies
The Content Authority + Paid Amplification Play
Trigger: SEO builds a high-quality piece of authority content (industry hub, compliance guide, material spec deep-dive) that earns strong engagement and early ranking signals but has low direct conversion volume.
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Build the content specifically to earn E-E-A-T signals: first-hand expertise, original data from Loop ICP patterns, specific buyer use cases the client has actually served
  • Structure with conversion pathways — the authority content should link to product pages, include CTAs for spec downloads or consultations, not just exist as a standalone informational page
  • Track organic impressions, clicks, engagement rate, and scroll depth — strong engagement signals to Google that the content deserves ranking even before it converts directly
  • Pass the content URL and engagement metrics to Paid as a remarketing asset candidate
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Build a remarketing audience from visitors to the authority content page — these buyers have demonstrated research intent beyond a standard product page visit
  • Serve retargeting ads to this authority content visitor audience with a stronger commercial CTA: "Ready to request a quote?" — they've already been educated, they need a conversion trigger
  • Test using the authority content as a paid landing page for specific informational queries — buyers searching "DFARS titanium requirements" may convert better landing on the compliance guide than a product page
⟳ Together
  • The organic content creates the buyer education layer. Paid monetizes the engaged audience that organic content produces. Neither channel alone completes the loop.
  • Report authority content performance as a combined metric: organic engagement + paid retargeting conversions from that audience = the true value of the content investment
  • If the authority content earns AI Overview citation, Paid should immediately build presence on the cited query to capture commercial intent that the zero-click citation creates
Combined outcome: SEO's content investment generates a qualified, already-educated audience that Paid converts at higher rates and lower CPAs than cold traffic. The content earns organic authority AND fuels paid conversion efficiency — two returns from one investment.
6
Play 6 — Both Channels Coordinate
The New Industry / ICP Expansion Play
Trigger: Loop ICP data shows a new industry or company type appearing in form submissions that the client wasn't deliberately targeting — an unplanned inbound demand signal worth capitalizing on.
🔍 SEO Strategist
  • Validate the industry demand — check GSC for impressions on industry-specific queries, run SERP analysis on the vertical's most common search patterns, and assess whether the client can actually serve this industry well
  • Build a dedicated industry landing page if validation confirms demand: industry-specific applications, relevant compliance certifications, case study language drawn from Loop submission patterns
  • Add industry-specific B-SMART® keyword clusters to the content pipeline: material + application + industry + requirement combinations that this vertical's buyers actually search
💰 Paid Media Strategist
  • Launch a targeted paid campaign for the new industry vertical while the organic page is being built — the inbound demand signal means buyers are already searching, Paid captures them immediately
  • Use the Loop ICP language from the new industry's submissions in ad copy — mirror the terminology these buyers used in their form comments for maximum relevance
  • Build a Customer Match audience from the new industry's submitters — this signals to PMax what the ICP profile looks like for this vertical and helps the algorithm find similar buyers
⟳ Together
  • Validate together with the Integrated Strategy Lead and client before full investment — can the client actually serve this industry? Are the margins right? Does the sales team know how to close this buyer type?
  • Share a 90-day build plan: Paid launches immediately, SEO builds organic authority over the next two quarters, combined SERP presence for the new vertical is the goal
  • Report the expansion to the client as evidence of Loop ICP intelligence working: "Our form data identified organic demand from this industry we weren't targeting — here's how both channels responded to capture it."
Combined outcome: An unplanned inbound demand signal from Loop gets converted into a deliberate, dual-channel expansion play within 30–60 days. The client grows into a new vertical with immediate paid presence and compounding organic authority — informed entirely by real buyer data, not keyword planner assumptions.

Every meeting should produce at minimum: one SEO action from paid data, and one paid action from SEO data.

Partnership Playbook Recap

What Every SEO and Paid Media Strategist Should Walk Away With

These are the principles that define an effective SEO + Paid partnership at OBx. They apply regardless of account size, vertical, or how long you've been working with a client.

01
You Share a Search Ecosystem
Both channels appear on the same SERP, compete for the same buyer attention, and drive toward the same conversion. Optimizing in isolation means optimizing with incomplete information — every single time.
02
Each Channel Sees What the Other Can't
Paid sees query-level conversion data and real-time competitive auction signals. SEO sees long-term authority patterns and SERP landscape shifts. Neither is complete without the other. The exchange is not optional.
03
The Buyer Journey Is Multi-Touch
65% of multi-touchpoint purchase journeys include Google or YouTube before Meta. Buyers research organically, return directly, and convert via paid — or vice versa. Last-click attribution lies to both channels equally.
04
Last-Click Isn't a Strategy, It's a Starting Point
GA4's default model systematically undervalues upper-funnel content and over-credits the final click. Use GA4 Conversion Paths, Data-Driven attribution, and Loop ICP form submissions together as a fuller picture of what's actually generating pipeline.
05
Bring Data the Other Channel Needs
The SEO Strategist brings ranking gaps, organic CTR wins, AI Overview audits, and Loop ICP language. The Paid Media Strategist brings search term reports, quality scores, auction insights, and negative keyword patterns. Every meeting. Every month.
06
Every Meeting Must Produce a Cross-Channel Action
A cross-channel review that produces no actions from the other channel's data produced no cross-channel value. The measure of success is simple: at least one SEO action from paid data, and at least one paid action from SEO data, every month.
